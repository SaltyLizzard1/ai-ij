# 90 days in Chiang Mai: the licence, the flat, and the one question that saved me weeks

## Arriving without a licence

I arrived without a driving licence. I haven't had one since 2020. In Thailand, car and motorcycle licences are separate, so I started with the motorcycle licence, because a scooter is how this city moves.

Yes, you can DIY the whole thing. You handle the paperwork yourself, wait in the queues, and communicate with the Department of Land Transport using Google Translate. I decided I wanted a little more hand-holding, so I asked around.

> Ask people who actually live here. The internet can tell you what something costs. People who have lived here can tell you what it actually costs.

## The test

The written test was translated directly into English, and some of the questions were... interesting. I failed the practice test the first time, practiced like crazy, and passed the next day.

The funny thing is, the practical part was the easy bit. Six weeks after landing I had a licence, a scooter, and a mountain road I ride most Sunday mornings.

## Finding a flat with a view

I wasn't sure I could live somewhere without the ocean. Sixty days later, I have a flat with a mountain view and a balcony where I drink coffee before the heat arrives.

What made the difference:

- Walking neighbourhoods on foot before looking at a single listing online.
- Asking in two local Facebook groups what people actually pay, not what agents quote.
- Saying no to the first three places, even though the first one was lovely.

## What the six years taught me

The first time I tried to make this permanent, I got stuck. The second time, I planned. The tool I built to plan the leap is what I use now, every week, to keep the business running from a laptop in a cafe.
